1.0
Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This Software Requirements Specification (SRS) defines the functional and non-functional requirements for a content-integrated e-commerce platform designed to unify content making, product discovery, and a purchase within a single user journey. The document follows the IEEE 830-1998 recommended practice for software requirements specifications.
This SRS serves as the reference for the development team (one frontend developer and three backend developers), for the academic supervisor evaluating the quality, and for any future maintainers of the system. It describes the release 1.0 MVP scope. Features designated as out of scope for the MVP are explicitly marked.
The document covers three user-facing subsystems:
B2C subsystem – the buyer-facing marketplace and social content feed.
B2B subsystem – the seller's workspace for content creation, product catalog management, order processing, and store configuration.
Administrative subsystem – the internal moderator and administrator interfaces (referenced where relevant, but detailed specification is deferred to a separate SRS, as internal roles are not part of the customer journey scope).

1.2 Document Conventions
The following conventions are used throughout this document:
Requirement identifiers follow the pattern REQ-<FEATURE>-<N> (e.g., REQ-AUTH-1), where <FEATURE> is a short mnemonic for the feature group and <N> is a sequential number within that group.
Priority levels are expressed as:
High – required for the MVP to be considered functional.
Medium – strongly desired; may be deferred if schedule pressure requires it.
Low – nice-to-have; scheduled for post-MVP.
Modal verbs follow RFC 2119 semantics:
Must – mandatory requirement.
Should – recommended, though exceptions are permitted with justification.
May – optional.
Placeholders marked as TBD (To Be Determined) indicate requirements that require further investigation before they can be finalized.
All UI text and user-facing terminology is written in English in this document; the initial product UI will be delivered in Russian (see Section 1.4).

1.4 Product Scope
The platform is a content-integrated e-commerce platform for handmade goods. It addresses a documented market gap: existing platforms separate content (maker blogs, posts, storytelling) from commerce (product catalog, cart, checkout), forcing buyers to leave the discovery context to complete a purchase and forcing sellers to promote products on external channels.
The platform's central value proposition is the continuous relationship between content, product discovery, and commerce, expressed in two core flows:
Buyer flow: Post → Product → Cart → Order
Seller flow: Create content → Link product → Receive order → Manage sale

2. Overall Description
2.1 Product Perspective
The Handmade Marketplace Platform is a new, self-contained product. The MVP is intended for academic demonstration and is not deployed to production; third-party payment and delivery integrations are implemented against sandbox environments.
The platform fits into the broader market context described in project proposal (reference 1.5.1), where existing competitors fall into three categories:
Content-strong, commerce-weak – Yarmarka Masterov (blogs separated from product cards; journal app distinct from marketplace).
Commerce-strong, content-absent – Ozon, Wildberries (no maker identity; factory competition on price).
Content-present, integration-absent – Lambda Market (Telegram-only content), Hands4U (blogs invisible to buyers).
The proposed platform's distinguishing architectural decision is that content and product are linked entities within a single system. Technologies below are referenced in paragraph 1.5.1 in the technology stack section.

The frontend (Next.js) communicates exclusively with the Core API over HTTPS. It never contacts PostgreSQL, Redis, YooKassa, or CDEK directly. The Core API mediates all persistence, third-party integration, and business-rule enforcement.
2.2 Product Functions
At a high level, the platform provides the following major functions:
2.2.1 Buyer-Facing Functions (B2C)
F1. OAuth2 registration and authentication via Yandex ID.
F2. Content feed discovery – browse a recommendation-driven feed of maker posts.
F3. Search and filtering across posts, products, and sellers.
F4. Seller public profile browsing with posts and product catalog tabs.
F5. Product detail viewing with sale mode, delivery options, and seller card.
F6. Seller follow/unfollow.
F7. Cart management with seller- and delivery-based grouping.
F8. Checkout with online payment (YooKassa) and delivery selection (CDEK or self-pickup).
F9. Order history and order detail viewing.
F10. Buyer profile management.
2.2.2 Seller-Facing Functions (B2B)
F11. Seller onboarding and store creation.
F12. Subscription tier selection (Free / Premium).
F13. Product catalog CRUD (create, edit, delete, stock and price management).
F14. Content creation – post composition with linked products.
F15. Order management – view incoming orders, update status, view delivery details.
F16. Delivery configuration – enable self-pickup locations and/or CDEK integration.
F17. Store profile management – display name, avatar, bio, and storefront preferences.
F18. Dashboard with operational summary (orders, views, followers).
2.2.3 Platform Functions
F19. Content–product linkage in the data model (a post may reference one or more products; a product page may surface related posts).
F20. Recommendation of posts on the buyer home feed (initially rule-based / recency-weighted; ML deferred).
F21. Integration with YooKassa for online payments and seller subscription billing.
F22. Integration with CDEK for shipping and order tracking.
2.3 User Classes and Characteristics
The platform serves four user classes, of which two are external (part of the customer journey) and two are internal (operational roles).
2.3.1 Buyers 
Profile. Women, age 25–44, seeking soulful, non-mass-produced items. They discover products through visual content, value the maker's story, and prefer to purchase without leaving the discovery context.
Characteristics.
Moderate technical proficiency; mobile-first browsing habits.
Motivated by curiosity, authenticity, and engagement with creators.
Value continuity: a purchase must be completed without navigating to a separate marketplace or social platform.
Primary success signal: a completed order reached from a content post.
Requirements that pertain specifically to buyers: F1-F10, F19, F20, F21.
2.3.2 Sellers 
Profile. Small business owners or self-employed creators producing handmade goods in small series. They currently manage content, promotion, and sales across fragmented channels (Instagram, Telegram, marketplaces).
Characteristics.
Moderate to high technical proficiency; accustomed to content tools and light analytics.
Operate solo or in very small teams; time-constrained.
Need integrated tooling: create content → link products → receive orders → manage delivery – without context switching.
Primary success signal: an order received without leaving the platform and without manual cross-channel coordination.
Requirements that pertain specifically to sellers: F11-F18, F19, F21, F22.
2.3.3 Administrators 
Profile. Platform operators responsible for user account management, dispute resolution, and configuration of platform-wide parameters (commission rates, subscription tier features).
Characteristics.
Full access to all platform data.
Requirements deferred to a separate Administrative SRS.
2.3.4 Moderators
Profile. Platform staff responsible for content moderation (post and product review), policy enforcement, and takedown of non-compliant listings.
Characteristics.
Read-mostly access to content and product entities.
Requirements deferred to a separate Administrative SRS.
2.3.5 Priority of User Classes
For the MVP, Buyers and Sellers are the primary user classes; both must be fully functional for the deliverable to be considered complete. Administrators and Moderators are secondary for this release and are acknowledged in the system design but not implemented.

2.4 Operating Environment
2.4.1 Client-Side Environment
Supported browsers: latest two version of Chrome (desktop and mobile).
Devices: desktop (≥1280px), tablet (768–1279px), mobile (≤767px).
Network: functional over HTTPS
JavaScript required for interactive features; no SSR-only fallback for the MVP.
2.4.2 Server-Side Environment
Container orchestration: Kubernetes.
Runtime: JVM (Kotlin, Spring Boot 3).
Database: PostgreSQL 16 (primary data store; relational model with JSONB for flexible fields, and tsvector for full-text search).
Cache: Redis (hot feed cache, view counters, session-adjacent data).
Deployment targets: Yandex Cloud (compute and manage PostgreSQL/Redis).
Observability: structured logging, health-check endpoints; detailed monitoring stack TBD.
2.4.3 Frontend Deployment
Framework: Next.js (App Router), TypeScript.
Rendering: server components for content-heavy pages; client components for interactive surfaces (cart, forms, feeds with infinite scroll).
Hosting: TBD (Vercel or Yandex Cloud static + edge).

2.5 Design and Implementation Constraints
2.5.1 Technology Constraints
Backend language is fixed to Kotlin with Spring Boot 3 to leverage strict typing, mature ecosystem, and first-class OAuth2 and third-party API integration.
PostgreSQL is the mandated primary data store. Alternative data stores (MongoDB, Elasticsearch) are not permitted for the MVP; full-text search must be implemented via tsvector.
Redis is mandated for caching of the hot feed and view counters; caching must not be implemented in the application layer to preserve horizontal scalability.
Kubernetes is the mandated deployment substrate to allow independent scaling of Core API, Search Service, and Recommendation Service.
Frontend must be built with Next.js and TypeScript. No alternative framework is in scope.
Authentication is delegated to Yandex ID via OAuth2. Storing passwords is explicitly prohibited. No SMTP configuration for password reset is required, as credential management is fully delegated to the identity provider.
2.5.2 Integration Constraints
Payment integration is limited to YooKassa, which is chosen because it provides both subscription billing (for Premium tier) and safe-deal fund holding (for online purchases). No alternative payment providers are in scope for the MVP.
Delivery integration is limited to CDEK, chosen because tracking-by-tracking-number enables automatic closure of the safe deal upon delivery. Self-pickup is offered as a non-integrated alternative.
External integrations are developed against sandbox environments for the MVP; no live production keys are used.
2.5.3 Organizational Constraints
Team composition is fixed: one frontend developer and three backend developers, over the project development and defense period.
No production deployment is required for the MVP. Deliverable is a demonstrable, functional system in a controlled environment.
2.5.4 Security Constraints
All client–server communication must be over HTTPS.
All third-party integrations must use OAuth2 or equivalent token-based delegation; no credentials may be stored in frontend code.
Personal data processing must comply with Russian Federal Law No. 152-FZ "On Personal Data."

2.7 Assumptions and Dependencies
2.7.1 Assumptions
Yandex ID remains available and stable as the sole authentication provider for the MVP. If the provider's API changes significantly during development, the authentication flow will require revision.
YooKassa sandbox provides sufficient fidelity to validate subscription billing and safe-deal fund holding without a production merchant account.
CDEK sandbox provides sufficient fidelity to validate shipment creation and tracking lookup.
The MVP scale is bounded at ~300,000 SKUs, as declared in the project proposal. Above this threshold, the current tsvector-based search and PostgreSQL configuration may require re-evaluation (e.g., dedicated search service).
User research is hypothesis-based. Both Customer Journey Maps (buyer and seller) were derived from secondary research, not validated with representative users. Post-MVP usability testing is expected to refine the requirements.
No traffic beyond academic demonstration is anticipated for the MVP; performance targets in Section 5 assume demonstrative, not commercial, load.

3. External Interface Requirements
3.1 User Interfaces
3.1.1 Design Principles
The user interface is designed around the platform's core value proposition – a continuous relationship between content, product discovery, and commerce. The following principles govern all UI decisions:
Content-to-commerce continuity. A buyer must be able to move from a content post to a completed purchase without leaving the platform and without losing context. Every post exposes its linked products inline; every product page exposes the seller's content.
Dual persona clarity. The B2C (buyer) and B2B (seller) interfaces are visually and structurally distinct. A user with both roles can switch between them without ambiguity about which mode they are in.
Progressive disclosure. Complex operations (e.g., product creation, post composition, checkout) are broken into steps or surfaces, not presented as single overwhelming forms.
Mobile-first B2C, desktop-first B2B. Buyers are expected to discover and purchase on mobile. Sellers are expected to manage catalog and content on desktop.
Optimistic feedback. Interactive actions (add-to-cart, follow, like) provide immediate visual feedback without waiting for server confirmation; the server response reconciles state asynchronously.
The UI implementation uses Next.js (App Router) with TypeScript, Tailwind CSS for styling, and shadcn/ui as the component primitive library. Icons are sourced from Lucide. Notifications are rendered via Sonner.

3.1.3 B2C (Buyer-Facing) Screens
Eleven screens comprise the B2C interface. All are reachable without authentication except where noted.
B2C-1: Home / Feed (/)
Purpose. Primary discovery surface. Presents a search bar and a vertically scrolling feed of recommended posts, each with linked product snippets.
Key elements:
Search bar (top) – navigates to /search?q=... on submit.
Feed – infinite-scrolling list of post cards.
Post card consists of:
Seller header: avatar, display name or username, follower count, "Follow" / "Following" button.
Post content: title and body text (truncated with "Read more" if long).
Optional cover image.
Linked products strip: horizontally scrollable list of product snippets.
Engagement row: like count, comment count.
Product snippet consists of: image, title, price, delivery method badge, and two action buttons – "Add to cart" and "Buy now" (the latter navigates directly to /checkout).
Clicking the product image or title (not the buttons) navigates to /product/[id].
Empty state: "Пока нет публикаций" with a link to search.
B2C-2: Search / Results (/search)
Purpose. Search results across three entity types, with tabbed navigation.
Key elements:
Search bar (top) – persistent, prefilled with the current query.
Three tabs: Posts | Products | Sellers.
Posts tab: grid or list of post cards (same structure as home feed, but without infinite scroll – paginated or "load more").
Products tab: grid of product cards with filters (price range, delivery method, sale mode, in-stock only).
Sellers tab: grid of seller cards (avatar, name, follower count, product count, "Follow" button).
No-results state: "Ничего не найдено. Попробуйте изменить запрос." with suggestions.
URL state is reflected in query parameters: ?q=...&tab=posts|products|sellers&page=N.
B2C-3: Seller Public Profile (/seller/[username])
Purpose. Public storefront for a seller. Reachable from post cards, product cards, and search results.
Key elements:
Seller header: avatar, display name, @username, bio, follower count, product count, "Follow" / "Following" button, rating (out of 5).
Search bar scoped to seller's products – filters the Products tab.
Two tabs: Posts | Products.
Posts tab: list of the seller's posts, same card format as home feed.
Products tab: grid of the seller's products with the seller-scoped search applied.
No-results / empty states for both tabs.
B2C-4: Product Detail (/product/[id])
Purpose. Full product information and primary purchase entry point.
Key elements:
Image gallery (left on desktop, top on mobile).
Product info (right on desktop):
Title, price, stock indicator (inStock).
Sale mode determines actions:
online – "Add to cart" and "Buy now" buttons; quantity selector; delivery method indicator.
out_of_stock – buttons disabled with message "Нет в наличии".
deal_only – no cart buttons; instead, a contact block displaying dealContacts text ("Свяжитесь с продавцом для оформления сделки").
Delivery options block:
If pickup – list of pickup points as {city} – {address}, multiple entries possible.
If cdek – label "Доставка СДЭК".
Seller card (bottom of the page) with five fields: avatar, name, rating, follower count, "Перейти в магазин" button linking to /seller/[username].
Related posts (optional, below seller card): posts from the same seller that link this product.
B2C-5: Cart (/cart)
Purpose. Review and group items before checkout. Requires authentication.
Key elements:
Groups – cart is grouped by (seller, delivery method) pair. Each group contains:
Seller header: avatar, name, delivery method label.
Available items – horizontally scrollable row of product snippets with quantity controls and remove action.
Unavailable items – same format, visually de-emphasized, with a "Remove" action and a reason label ("Нет в наличии" or "Только по сделке").
"К оформлению" button – proceeds to checkout for this group only.
Empty state: "Ваша корзина пуста" with a link to the feed.
Cart summary (bottom sticky bar): total items, total price across all groups.
B2C-6: Checkout (/checkout)
Purpose. Confirm delivery and pay. Requires authentication.
Key elements:
Grouped display by seller and delivery method (same grouping as cart).
Delivery method: displayed as a label (not editable here – determined by the group's products).
Item list: compact list without images – quantity × title × unit price = line total.
Pickup point selector (only if pickup): radio list of pickup points to choose.
Order summary: subtotal, delivery cost (if applicable), total to pay.
"Оплатить" button – initiates payment via YooKassa. Button state during processing: disabled with spinner.
Cancel link – returns to cart without losing selection.
B2C-7: Buyer Profile (/account)
Purpose. Manage personal account.
Key elements:
Profile header: avatar, display name, @username, email (read-only, from Yandex ID), registration date.
Editable fields: display name, avatar, bio.
Role management: if the user is not yet a seller, a prominent "Стать продавцом" button that initiates the seller onboarding flow.
Navigation links: "Мои заказы" → /account/orders; "Выйти" → clears session and redirects to /.
B2C-8: Buyer Orders List (/account/orders)
Purpose. List of the buyer's orders.
Key elements:
Order cards – each shows: order number, date, seller, delivery method, total, status badge, first item's thumbnail, and a "Подробнее" link to /account/orders/[id].
Status filter tabs: All | Active | Completed | Cancelled.
Pagination or "load more".
Empty state: "У вас пока нет заказов" with a link to the feed.
B2C-9: Buyer Order Detail (/account/orders/[id])
Purpose. Full information about a single order.
Key elements:
Header: order number, status badge, date.
Delivery block: delivery method, pickup point (if pickup), tracking number (if CDEK and available).
Items list: product snippets with quantity and price.
Total block: subtotal, delivery, total.
Seller card: link to /seller/[username].
Actions: "Повторить заказ" (re-adds items to cart, if available); "Отменить" (only if status is created or paid).
B2C-10: Login (/login)
Purpose. Authentication entry point.
Key elements:
Centered card with platform logo.
"Войти через Яндекс" button – initiates OAuth2 flow via Yandex ID. No email/password form (delegated to Yandex).
Link to register ("Впервые здесь? Зарегистрироваться").
Error state: if OAuth callback fails, display error message and retry button.
B2C-11: Registration (/register)
Purpose. Account creation. Because authentication is delegated to Yandex ID, registration is effectively the first successful OAuth flow plus a role-selection step.
Key elements:
Centered card with platform logo.
"Продолжить с Яндекс" button – same OAuth2 flow as login.
Intent parameter: if URL contains ?intent=seller, the post-registration redirect goes to /seller/onboarding; otherwise to /account.
Terms of service checkbox (required).
Link to login ("Уже есть аккаунт? Войти").
3.1.4 B2B (Seller-Facing) Screens
Eleven screens comprise the B2B interface. All require authentication with the seller role; unauthorized access is redirected to /seller/onboarding if the user is authenticated but not yet a seller, or to /login if not authenticated.
B2B-1: Dashboard (/seller)
Purpose. Operational summary for the seller. Default landing page after login.
Key elements:
Four summary cards: new orders count, unshipped orders count, total product views (last 30 days), follower count.
Recent orders list – last 5 orders with status badges and a link to each.
Low-stock alerts – list of products with inStock below a threshold (e.g., 3), with quick links to edit.
Quick action buttons: "Добавить товар", "Создать пост".
B2B-2: Onboarding (/seller/onboarding)
Purpose. Guide new sellers through store setup. Displayed once after the first "become a seller" action; subsequently available from the sidebar.
Key elements:
Textual introduction to platform capabilities.
Checklist:
Create a store profile (name, avatar, bio).
Add first product.
Publish first post.
Configure delivery.
Each item is clickable and navigates to the corresponding screen. Completed items are marked with a checkmark.
"Перейти в кабинет" button when the profile is created (minimum requirement to access the rest of B2B).

3.4 Communications Interfaces
The platform is a web-based application in which communication between the frontend, backend services, and external systems is performed over standard HTTP-based protocols.
3.4.1 Client-Server Communication
The web frontend communicates with the backend exclusively through HTTPS. The frontend does not access PostgreSQL, Redis, YooKassa, CDEK, or other internal services directly.
The following communication requirements apply:
All client–server communication shall use HTTPS.
HTTP API requests and responses shall use JSON as the primary message format.
REST-style HTTP methods shall be used according to the operation being performed: GET for data retrieval, POST for creation or actions, PATCH and PUT for updates, and DELETE for removal.
API endpoints shall use the /api/v1 version prefix.
B2C page endpoints shall use the /api/v1/pages prefix and return structured page models.
API error responses shall use a unified structure containing an error code, message, details, and request identifier.
The system shall support X-Request-Id propagation for tracing a request across backend services.
Authentication in production shall use a JWT transmitted through the Authorization: Bearer HTTP header. The development environment may use the X-User-Id header as specified in Section 4.1.10.
3.4.2 Communication with External Services
The platform communicates with external services through their provided APIs.
Yandex ID. Authentication is performed through the OAuth2 authorization flow provided by Yandex ID. User credentials are not stored by the platform.
YooKassa. Payment and subscription-related operations communicate with YooKassa through its API. The platform shall also be able to receive payment-related notifications from YooKassa through webhooks. External payment communication shall use authenticated API requests and shall not expose payment credentials to the frontend.
CDEK. Delivery-related operations communicate with CDEK through the CDEK API. Requests and responses shall comply with the communication format and protocol defined by CDEK. Tracking information may be obtained through the CDEK API using the corresponding shipment or tracking identifier.
External integrations for the MVP shall use sandbox environments rather than live production credentials.
3.4.3 Internal Service Communication
The B2C and B2B subsystems communicate with data-owning backend services through HTTP APIs. The B2C module acts as an intermediary between the frontend and upstream services such as Users, Goods, Publications, Cart, Orders, Search, and Eventer. Independent upstream requests may be executed in parallel when assembling a page. If a non-critical upstream service is unavailable, the corresponding page section shall be marked as unavailable without preventing the remaining page content from being returned. The frontend shall not communicate directly with upstream backend services.
3.4.4 Security and Data Transfer
All network communication containing user, order, authentication, or other sensitive information shall be protected using HTTPS/TLS. The platform shall use TLS 1.2 or higher as specified by the security requirements. Credentials, access tokens, and other confidential integration data shall not be embedded in frontend code or exposed through API responses. No fixed network bandwidth or data transfer rate is specified for the MVP. Performance expectations for user-visible requests are defined separately in Section 5.

4. System Features
4.1 System Features of the Buyer-Facing Part (B2C) 
This section specifies the functional requirements for the buyer-facing (B2C) part of the handmade goods marketplace. B2C covers everything the buyer needs to browse the catalog, engage with master content, manage the cart, and place orders. Seller-side (B2B) capabilities - creating products, publications, and master profiles - are out of scope for this chapter: that data enters the platform from elsewhere and is available to B2C only through read models and events.
Architectural principle: the web client talks to a single central B2C module (Backend-for-Frontend). For each frontend screen, the module exposes one GET endpoint that returns a typed page model (metadata, sections, cards). Mutations (cart, subscriptions, checkout, event submission) use separate action endpoints on the same module. B2C aggregates data from the users, publications, cart, orders, goods, search, and eventer services, plus the Gorse recommendation engine; the frontend has no direct access to upstream services.
4.1.1 Central B2C Module (Single Entry Point for the Buyer) 
4.1.1.1 Description and Priority
The central B2C module acts as a page orchestrator: it accepts HTTP requests from the client, resolves the user context, fetches data from specialized services in parallel, maps responses into a unified contract of cards and sections, and returns a result suitable for direct UI rendering without additional assembly on the frontend.
Priority: High.

4.1.1.3 Functional Requirements
REQ-4.1.1-01: B2C shall be the only component exposed to the buyer web client for B2C business operations (pages and actions), except static assets and external payment widgets (if added later).
REQ-4.1.1-02: All page endpoints shall use the prefix /api/v1/pages and return JSON that conforms to Pydantic page models (documented in the B2C module OpenAPI).
REQ-4.1.1-03: All action endpoints shall use the prefix /api/v1 (without pages) and shall not duplicate data-ownership logic: writes are performed in the owning service (cart, orders, users); B2C coordinates call sequences.
REQ-4.1.1-04: Error responses shall use a unified structure: { "error": { "code", "message", "details", "request_id" } }.
REQ-4.1.1-05: B2C shall propagate or generate X-Request-Id for end-to-end request tracing to upstreams.
REQ-4.1.1-06: Page assembly shall use parallel execution of independent upstream requests (gather_sections).
REQ-4.1.1-07: Each non-critical page section shall have a status field with values ok, empty, or unavailable, and a list of items (or an empty list when degraded).
REQ-4.1.1-08: Data critical to the page (entity id in the URL) that is missing from the catalog shall result in HTTP 404, not a partial page with an empty body.
REQ-4.1.1-09: B2C shall not perform cross-service database JOINs; enrichment (hydration) shall be done only via HTTP APIs of data owners (e.g., batch products after search or recommendations).
REQ-4.1.1-10: Contracts for shared UI blocks (ProductCard, MasterCard, PublicationCard, PriceBlock, PageMeta, CategoryChip) shall remain stable across pages.

4.2 System Features of of the Seller-Facing Part (B2B)
This section specifies seller-facing system features of the handmade marketplace. Each feature is described using the same three-part pattern as Section 4.1: Description and Priority; Stimulus/Response Sequences; Functional Requirements.
Scope: B2B owns seller stores, product catalogues, publications and access-controlled seller operations. Shared User, Order, Payment and Delivery services own their respective domain logic. B2B may retrieve permitted information from them or submit authorized requests.
API contract status: All /api/v1/seller/... paths shown below are illustrative proposed routes (TBD), not approved implementation endpoints. They are included to align presentation with the B2C section; the final routes, payloads and response codes must be agreed in OpenAPI/Swagger. UI paths such as /seller/products are not API routes.
4.2.1 Seller Access, Registration and Onboarding
4.2.1.1 Description and Priority
This feature allows an authenticated platform user to enter the seller workspace or register as a seller and create a store. User credentials and global user roles are maintained by shared platform services; the B2B subsystem checks the confirmed authorization context and manages the store profile. A user with buyer and seller roles may access both corresponding interfaces.

4.2.1.2 Stimulus/Response Sequences

Stimulus
Response
S1
An authenticated seller opens the seller workspace.
The B2B subsystem verifies the seller role and returns accessible store information. GET /api/v1/seller/me - 200.
S2
An authenticated user without a seller profile chooses “Become a Seller”.
The system presents onboarding and allows the user to submit store details POST /api/v1/seller/onboarding.
S3
The user submits valid, unique store details.
The system verifies eligibility and creates the store profile; returns the store identifier and registration result (201 after confirmation).
S4
The user submits missing fields or an occupied username.
The system rejects the request with field-specific validation or conflict information (400/409); no duplicate store profile is created.
S5
An unauthenticated or unauthorized user requests protected seller functionality.
The system rejects access (401/403) and does not expose protected store data.
S6
An existing seller attempts registration again.
The system returns the existing registration state rather than creating a duplicate seller profile.

4.2.1.3 Functional Requirements
REQ-4.2.1-01 (High). The B2B subsystem shall identify the authenticated user and verify the seller role when protected seller workspace functions are accessed.
REQ-4.2.1-02 (High). The system shall grant access to store management operations only to users with the required seller permissions.
REQ-4.2.1-03 (High). If a user is authenticated but does not have the seller role, the system shall allow the user to initiate seller registration.
REQ-4.2.1-04 (High). The system shall support access to the seller workspace for users who have both buyer and seller roles.
REQ-4.2.1-05 (High). The system shall reject access to protected B2B operations if authentication has not been confirmed.
REQ-4.2.1-06 (High). The B2B subsystem shall not grant access to another seller's store solely on the basis of a store identifier supplied by the user.
REQ-4.2.1-07 (High). The B2B subsystem shall not change passwords or other account credentials managed by the shared User Service.
REQ-4.2.1-08 (High). The system shall allow an authenticated user to create a seller profile.
REQ-4.2.1-09 (High). When creating a store, the system shall accept a store name, unique username, and other required profile information.
REQ-4.2.1-10 (High). The system shall verify that all required fields are completed before confirming registration.
REQ-4.2.1-11 (High). Each created store shall have a unique identifier and an association with the authenticated owner.
REQ-4.2.1-12 (High). The system shall verify the uniqueness of the username used in the store's public address.
REQ-4.2.1-13 (High). The system shall prevent duplicate seller-profile registration for an already registered user; if that user is eligible for multiple stores, additional store creation shall follow the separately agreed store-management rules.
REQ-4.2.1-14 (High). After successful registration, the system shall grant the seller access to further store setup.
REQ-4.2.1-15 (Medium). The system should provide the seller with an initial store setup checklist indicating completed steps.
REQ-4.2.1-16 (Medium). The system should allow sellers to reopen the initial setup instructions.

S4
Another user requests changes to a store they do not control.
The system denies the action and does not modify settings.
S5
An external configuration dependency rejects an update.
The B2B subsystem reports failure and does not falsely confirm a saved change.

4.2.9.3 Functional Requirements
REQ-4.2.9-01 (High). The system shall allow sellers to retrieve the current settings for the delivery methods available for their store.
REQ-4.2.9-02 (High). The system shall allow sellers to enable and disable delivery methods supported by the platform, within the permitted settings.
REQ-4.2.9-03 (High). When pickup is enabled, the system shall allow the seller to provide the supported pickup address fields.
REQ-4.2.9-04 (High). The system shall validate mandatory configuration fields before saving.
REQ-4.2.9-05 (High). The system shall reject changes to another store's delivery settings if the user lacks the required permissions.

4.2.10 Shared Validation and Error Responses
4.2.10.1 Description and Priority
The B2B subsystem applies consistent authorization, validation and error rules to all seller-facing operations. This cross-cutting section specifies expected externally observable behavior rather than an additional seller screen.

4.2.10.2 Stimulus/Response Sequences

Stimulus
Response
S1
A request contains invalid or missing required fields.
The system rejects it with machine-readable validation information; it preserves existing confirmed data.
S2
An unauthenticated user requests a protected resource.
The system returns an authentication failure (401).
S3
An authenticated seller requests another store’s resource.
The system rejects the operation (403 or safe non-disclosure handling); no unauthorized data are returned.
S4
The requested resource does not exist.
The system returns not-found (404).
S5
A required dependent service is unavailable.
The response distinguishes dependency failure from invalid user input; success is not falsely confirmed.
S6
A server error occurs.
The system returns a safe error description without exposing credentials, internal configuration or stack traces.

4.2.10.3 Functional Requirements
REQ-4.2.10-01 (High). Before executing a protected operation, the system shall verify the validity of the authentication context and the user's permissions.
REQ-4.2.10-02 (High). If invalid input data are received, the system shall reject the operation and return information about the errors detected.
REQ-4.2.10-03 (High). If the requested resource does not exist, the system shall return a resource-not-found message.
REQ-4.2.10-04 (High). If the user does not have permission to perform the requested operation, the system shall reject it without modifying data.
REQ-4.2.10-05 (High). The system shall not confirm successful completion of an operation if a required action was not performed.
REQ-4.2.10-06 (High). If a request to an external service fails, the system shall return a result that distinguishes service unavailability from invalid user input.
REQ-4.2.10-07 (High). Error messages shall not disclose credentials, confidential connection information, or internal server configuration parameters.
REQ-4.2.10-08 (High). If a data modification request is rejected, the previously confirmed state of the affected resource shall be preserved.
REQ-4.2.10-09 (Medium). The system should provide the frontend with information that allows users to retain data entered when validation fails and to resubmit a corrected request.

REQ-4.3-01: The system must create an order when a user initiates checkout for a pickup order.
REQ-4.3-02: The system must create a YuKassa Safe Deal for an order upon checkout.

REQ-4.3-04: The system must allow a seller to confirm stock availability for an order.
REQ-4.3-05: The system must allow a seller to confirm the item is ready at the pickup point.

REQ-4.3-07: The system must allow a seller to enter the buyer's pickup code to confirm fulfillment.
REQ-4.3-08: The system must initiate the closure of the YuKassa Safe Deal when the order is fulfilled.
REQ-4.3-09: The system must update the order after the seller receives the payout from YuKassa.
REQ-4.3-10: The system must handle a buyer payment interruption by either cancelling the order or, if a deal was created, requesting its cancellation via the YuKassa API and then cancelling the order.
REQ-4.3-11: The system must allow a buyer to cancel an order before seller confirmation, triggering a YuKassa Safe Deal cancellation and a refund if payment was made.
REQ-4.3-12: The system must allow a seller to cancel an order due to lack of stock, triggering a YuKassa Safe Deal cancellation, resetting product stock, and applying a penalty to the seller.
REQ-4.3-13: The system must allow a seller to cancel an order upon buyer request after confirmation, using the same cancellation flow as for lack of stock.
REQ-4.3-14: The system must create an order when a user initiates checkout for a CDEK delivery order.
REQ-4.3-15: The system must allow a seller to confirm stock availability, calculate the delivery cost via the CDEK API, and generate an invoice for the buyer including both the item and delivery costs.
REQ-4.3-16: The system must create a YuKassa Safe Deal after the seller confirms the order and invoice.
REQ-4.3-17: The system must process a buyer payment for the item and delivery through YuKassa and update the order accordingly.
REQ-4.3-18: The system must allow a seller to register a shipment with the CDEK API and attach a tracking number.
REQ-4.3-19: The system must integrate with the CDEK API to receive tracking status updates and update the order when the shipment arrives at the destination.
REQ-4.3-20: The system must integrate with the CDEK API to detect when the buyer has picked up the order and update the order accordingly.
REQ-4.3-21: The system must update the order after the seller receives the payout for a CDEK delivery order.
REQ-4.3-22: The system must provide an administrative interface to view and manage all orders and their current statuses.
REQ-4.3-23: The system must handle post-fulfillment return requests directly between the buyer and seller, outside the platform's automated processing flow.

Appendix A: Glossary
This glossary defines the main domain, architectural, technical, and product terms used throughout the SRS. 
Term
Definition
API (Application Programming Interface)
A set of endpoints and contracts used by software components to communicate with each other.
B2B subsystem
The seller-facing part of the platform used for store management, product catalog management, publications, orders, delivery settings, and subscription plans.
B2C subsystem
The buyer-facing part of the platform used for content discovery, product browsing, subscriptions, cart management, checkout, and order history.
BFF (Backend-for-Frontend)
A backend component that aggregates data from multiple backend services and returns a page-oriented response adapted to the frontend. In this SRS, the central B2C module acts as a BFF.
Buyer
A platform user who browses handmade products and publications, follows sellers, manages a cart, and places orders.
CDEK
A third-party delivery service integrated with the platform for shipment creation and delivery tracking.
Core API
The central backend entry point that mediates communication between the frontend and backend business services.
CRUD (Create, Read, Update, Delete)
The four basic operations used to manage persistent entities such as products and publications.
Degradation
A controlled behavior in which a non-critical page section becomes unavailable while the rest of the page remains functional.
Eventer
A backend service that receives behavioral events from the B2C module and forwards or stores them for analytics and recommendation processing.
Feed
A continuously browsable collection of publications or recommendations displayed to the buyer.
Gorse
A recommendation engine used to generate product or content recommendations based on user interactions and feedback events.
Hydration
The process of enriching identifiers returned by one service with full entity data obtained from another service, for example, resolving product IDs into product cards.
Idempotency-Key
An HTTP request header used to ensure that retrying an order creation request does not create duplicate orders.
JWT (JSON Web Token)
A token format used in production to identify and authenticate users through the Authorization: Bearer header.
Kafka
A distributed event-streaming platform used in the behavioral event processing pipeline.
Master
The buyer-facing term used in B2C models for a seller or handmade creator. A master has a public profile, products, publications, and followers.
MVP (Minimum Viable Product)
The initial release containing the functionality required to demonstrate the platform and satisfy the project scope.
OAuth2
An authorization framework used in the platform for delegated authentication and integration with external identity or service providers.
OpenAPI / Swagger
A standard and toolset for describing HTTP API contracts and generating interactive API documentation.
Page Model
A structured response returned by a B2C page endpoint, containing metadata, sections, cards, and other data required to render a page.
Post / Publication
Content created by a seller and shown to buyers. A publication may contain text, an optional image, and links to one or more products.
Premium Tier
A paid seller subscription plan that provides additional platform capabilities compared with the Free Tier.
Product
A handmade item offered by a seller through the platform. A product may have a price, stock quantity, images, delivery method, and sale mode.
Redis
An in-memory data store used for caching, streams, and other performance-related data in the platform.
Safe deal
A payment mechanism in which funds associated with an online order are held by the payment provider and related actions depend on the confirmed order or delivery state.
Sale mode
The mode determining how a product can be purchased. In the current MVP specification the supported modes are online, out_of_stock, and deal_only.
Sandbox environment
A non-production environment provided by an external service for testing integrations without using live production credentials or transactions.
Seller
A creator, small business owner, or self-employed user who operates a store and offers handmade products on the platform.
Subscription
A buyer's relationship with a master that allows the buyer to follow the master's new products and publications.
tsvector
A PostgreSQL data type used by the platform to support full-text search.
Upstream service
A backend service from which the B2C or B2B subsystem retrieves information or to which it sends authorized operations.
Yandex ID
The external identity provider used by the platform for user authentication through OAuth2.
YooKassa
The external payment provider used for online payments and seller subscription billing.
